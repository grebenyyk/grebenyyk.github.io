People don't care

Posted on Jun 27, 2026

What I think is largely missing from the online discourse on AI slop, is that most people don’t really care. You, reader, may think: “But I am not most people, I care a lot!” That may be true — you may care about your own work and others, and despise that slop is spreading into everything (code, creative writing, design). But most people don’t. Slop is already abundant; once it reads as the new normal, most people will simply accept it and not question it too much.

One relevant example is Russian personal names. In the Russian tradition, as well as in most European countries, the given name comes first, the family name comes second: Fedor Dostoevsky, Sergey Rachmaninov. In school, teachers usually call students by their family name first, for easier identification. Somehow that carried over into adulthood: when introducing themselves people would call themselves that: Ivanov Sergey, Kuznetsov Ivan. They don’t seem to care that it is wrong, or that it can even sound derogatory — the way a teacher might call on you when scolding you. And if people don’t care what to call themselves, why would they care that some texts or images on the internet are not up to your high standards of the pre-AI era?

Another example is the material culture around us that is becoming cheaper and more utilitarian with each new generation, losing the appeal and beauty of the old days. This is discussed quite a lot in the context of craftsmanship and that the pieces of knowledge work that are done by a human in the AI-era have this additional craftsmanship value. That is true, but on the other hand, things done by AI are not as inherently bad as one might think. They are okay, just like a mass-market piece of apparel is okay. People who know the industry see the difference, and care. Most people don’t.
